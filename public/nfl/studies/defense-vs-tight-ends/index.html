Is any defense actually “weak against tight ends”?

Verdict — Not a real trait

No. Tight-end yards allowed per target is the least repeatable defensive statistic we have measured. What a defense did to tight ends in the first half of a season tells you essentially nothing about the second half.

It is one of the most cited reasons to start a tight end — the matchup chart says a defense gives up the eighth-most points to the position, so you start yours. The problem is that the ranking barely survives contact with the rest of the same season.

Two different questions hide inside “weak against tight ends,” and only one of them has an answer. Which positions a defense concedes throws to is partly a scheme decision, and scheme decisions persist. How many yards those throws gain depends on the receiver, the quarterback and the bounce of the ball.

So if you use a defensive tendency at all, use target share allowed, not yards allowed. And do not let “good tight-end matchup” be the reason you start someone — it is the single least reproducible claim in this dataset, and one of the most repeated.

How this was measured

Every pass thrown to a tight end from 2018 through 2025, grouped by defense and season, from official play-by-play. Each defense-season is split into odd and even weeks and the halves correlated across defenses. Defense-seasons needed at least 20 tight-end targets in each half. Split-half r = +0.021; corrected for half-length, +0.040.

n=256 defense-seasons · 2018–2025 · play-by-play · last verified 22 September 2026
