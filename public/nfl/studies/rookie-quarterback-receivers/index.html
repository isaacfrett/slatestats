Does a rookie quarterback hurt his receivers?

Verdict — No measurable effect

Rookie quarterbacks really are worse — noticeably so. It does not reach their receivers’ reception totals at any position.

A rookie quarterback is roughly 0.13 expected points per play worse than a third-year one, which is a large gap. The natural conclusion is to fade his pass-catchers.

PositionRookie-QB weeksVeteran weeksDifferencet
Wide receivers1,57815,810−0.020−0.39
Tight ends9639,553−0.032−0.61
Running backs8979,264−0.051−0.85
All pass-catchers3,43834,627−0.031−0.98

How that can be true. There are two effects and they cancel. Rookie quarterbacks complete a meaningfully lower share of their throws — but their receivers draw a slightly larger share of the team’s targets. More looks at a worse completion rate lands in the same place.

This is about catches, not yards or efficiency. The quarterback gap is real and has to surface somewhere. It does not surface here.

How this was measured

Every pass-catcher week from 2018 to 2025 compared against his own prior four games, split by whether the team’s primary passer was in his first NFL season. Paired within player.

n=38,065 player-weeks · 2018–2025 · paired within player · last verified 22 September 2026
