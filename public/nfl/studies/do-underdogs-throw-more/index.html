Do underdogs really throw more?

Verdict — Barely — favorites run more

Passing volume moves about two plays across the entire spread range, and not even in a straight line. Rushing volume moves nearly twice as much and moves cleanly. The real effect is not that trailing teams throw — it is that leading teams run.

The reasoning is intuitive: a team expected to trail will have to pass to catch up, so its receivers see more volume. The spread is one of the most commonly used inputs in a start-sit decision.

Note the last row of the table. Big underdogs throw less than moderate underdogs — a team down four scores is not running a two-minute drill for sixty minutes, it is emptying the bench. The passing column is not monotonic, which is the tell that the effect is weak.

What this changes. The spread is a reasonable input for a rushing projection and close to useless for a receiving one, which is the opposite of how it is usually applied. Starting a receiver because his team is expected to trail is acting on about one extra pass play spread across five eligible receivers.

How this was measured

Every team-game from 2018 to 2025 with a closing point spread, pass and run plays counted from play-by-play. Buckets are the PRE-GAME spread, so nothing depends on knowing the result.

n=4,422 team-games · 2018–2025 · pre-game spread, no lookahead · last verified 22 September 2026
