If Tyler Warren is out, who gets his targets?

The Indianapolis Colts room absorbs +0.95 receptions, and Mo Alie-Cox takes the largest single share at +0.60. The work stays overwhelmingly inside the tight end room.

Where the work goes

Change in projected receptions for every other player in the offence. The bars are small on purpose — a vacated role is shared out, and any tool showing one replacement absorbing a whole target load is telling you something the data does not support.

The room total is predictable. Which player takes it is not. Every candidate predictor of who gains — pecking order, snap share, route depth, trailing targets — correlates at roughly r = 0.02. In any single week one player usually takes about 60% of what was vacated; it is simply not predictably the same one.
