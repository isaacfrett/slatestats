When does a player on IR change from Out to Questionable or Active?

Reserve is a roster move, not a weekly status, and it does not become Questionable — it ends when the team activates him.

These are two different systems and conflating them is the usual source of confusion. The weekly injury report — Out, Doubtful, Questionable — describes one upcoming game and is filed fresh each week. Reserve/Injured is a roster designation: the player is off the active roster entirely and is not on the weekly report at all, because he is not a candidate to play.

So a player on IR does not drift from Out to Questionable. He stays on reserve until the team formally activates him, and only once he is back on the active roster does he start appearing on the weekly report again — at which point he may well be listed Questionable for his first game back.

The NFL requires a minimum absence before a player can return from reserve, and teams get a limited number of activations per season. Both of those have changed more than once in recent years, so check the current rule rather than relying on a number you remember.

A practical consequence for lineups: because reserve persists until activation, yesterday’s roster tells you today’s availability. A weekly injury designation does not — a Questionable tag from last week says nothing about this week’s game, and carrying it forward is a common way to get a lineup wrong.

Your fantasy platform’s IR slot is a separate thing again. How many slots you get, and which real-life designations qualify a player for one, is a league setting that differs between platforms — check yours.
