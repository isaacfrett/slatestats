Is the year three breakout real?

Verdict — Year two is the only real jump

No. A receiver going into his third season gains +0.021 catches a game — nothing. The one real step up is year one to year two (+0.415), and decline sets in from year five.

“He’s entering year three” is the most repeated argument in dynasty football, and the curve that supports it is built the wrong way round. Average every receiver at each experience year and production climbs into his thirties — because the receivers still playing at thirty are the ones who were good enough to still be playing.

The year-two gain is a role change, not improvement. Decomposed, a second-year receiver picks up 7.4 points of snap share and is no better per target. He is not a better player; he is on the field more. Project his snaps and you have projected him.

What the cross-sectional curve is really showing. A receiver in his tenth season averages more than one in his third, and that is true and useless: the tenth-year population has had seven more chances to be cut. Every year of experience filters the group a little further toward the good ones, and the line drifts upward on nothing but the filtering.

The practical reading is narrow and worth having. Pay for the second-year step, because it is real and it is visible in snap share before it shows up in the box score. Do not pay a premium for the third.

How this was measured

Every receiver with at least six games in two consecutive seasons, 2016–2025, compared against HIMSELF — year N against year N+1, per game. Paired within player, so the survivorship that lifts the cross-sectional curve cannot reach it. The comparison figure on the card is the same step measured the usual way, by averaging whoever happened to be in each experience year.
