Do defenses shut down the other team’s best receiver?

Verdict — Barely

Hardly at all. Against the best quarter of pass defenses a team’s leading receiver catches 4.55 passes; against the worst quarter, 5.52. That is about one reception every other game between the two extremes of the league.

The shadow-corner fade is one of the oldest pieces of advice in fantasy football: a great defense is coming, so sit your best receiver. The effect exists. It is much smaller than the confidence with which it is given.

Offenses feed their best player regardless of who is across from him. Target concentration barely moves against a good defense, which is the mechanism: the ball still goes to the same man, it just gains slightly less when it gets there.

So a “tough matchup” is worth about a tenth of a reception on a lineup decision. If a market or a ranking shades a top receiver heavily for a matchup, it is moving further than the evidence supports.

How this was measured

A team’s leading receiver is identified from trailing target share before the game, never from the game itself. Opposing defenses are split into quarters within each season by receiving yards allowed. 2018–2025.
